Every water damage restoration job starts with extraction, and no other early step matters more. The faster standing water is removed from your property, the less damage occurs to structural materials, flooring, personal belongings and building systems. Left standing, water keeps moving deeper by the hour, wicking into drywall, soaking carpet pad, seeping under flooring and saturating the framing. For emergency water extraction in Peoria, Peoria Restoration Co brings truck-mounted units and portable extractors built to pull out hundreds of gallons every minute.

Truck-mounted units rank with the strongest water removal systems made, and their suction lifts water out of hard floors, carpet and structural materials much better than anything you can rent or buy. Where the truck hoses will not reach, we bring in submersible pumps, strong portable extractors and specialty tools made for water caught between floor layers, inside wall cavities or in tight spaces.

There is more to a professional extraction than getting rid of the water in plain sight. Our IICRC-certified technicians use moisture detection equipment to identify water that has migrated into hidden areas: behind baseboards, inside wall cavities, under cabinets and beneath flooring materials. Before drying starts, we use specialty tools to pull as much moisture as we can out of those hidden spots, so structural drying takes far less time and equipment and secondary mold is less likely.

  • A clearly labeled main water valve, in a spot everyone knows, saves time in an emergency
  • Place leak detection sensors in the key risk areas and test that they work
  • Leak-sensing shutoff valves can cut off the water on their own when something fails

Signs It Is Time for Water Extraction

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water flowing or dripping from ceilings, walls or fixtures
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Rising water in the basement because the sump pump stopped working
  • Pooled water on the carpet, or a soggy feel with every step
  • Water squeezing up between floorboards, planks or tiles when you walk
  • Any room where water has collected on the floor
  • Floors flooded by a fire sprinkler or suppression system going off
