A dry house is not yet a finished house. After readings return to normal, the bare subfloor, cut-out drywall and missing floors still need attention. Rebuilding all of that is what water damage repair means. Our team in Peoria replaces what could not be saved and matches it to what was there before, so the room looks like a room again and not a job site.

Repair work covers a wide range. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. Large jobs can include insulation, whole drywall sheets, subfloor, laminate or hardwood, cabinet boxes, doors and trim. Our aim is a like-for-like replacement, and when a true match cannot be found we say so directly and let you decide.

We do not close anything in while it is still damp. A moisture meter has to show normal levels in the subfloor and framing before new material is installed. Building over damp wood is how mold ends up behind a freshly painted wall. You also receive a written scope and schedule ahead of the work, which spells out what gets rebuilt and when.

How We Handle Water Damage Repair

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Verify Dryness First

Our crew meters the remaining drywall, the subfloor and the framing one last time. Only when each material reads normal does rebuilding start, which keeps damp spots from being sealed inside.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • During drying, sections of wallboard came out and the framing is still exposed.
  • Walking over the formerly wet area, you feel soft spots in the floor.
  • Grout is breaking up and some tiles have lifted where moisture reached the floor below.
  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • A door frame or slab swelled, so the door rubs or fails to close.
  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.

How to Prevent Water Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Store pictures of the finished work alongside the scope for future reference.
  • Keep leftover flooring, tile and paint details so future repairs can be matched.
  • Fix the source first, and only then patch or paint a water stain.
  • Take pictures of damaged materials prior to removal, since the insurer may want them later.
  • Use moisture resistant drywall and backer board when rebuilding bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms.
  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
